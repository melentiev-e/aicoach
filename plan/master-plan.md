### Vacation / Mountain-Maintenance Block (Aug 18 – Sep 6)  *(added 2026-07-30)*

**Reframe first:** this is not "three lost weeks." Three big Carpathian hikes
(Pip Ivan 18–20 km / +1200 m / 9–10 h; Shpytsi 15–16 km / +750 m / 6–7 h; Borzhava
ridge, easy panoramic) are large aerobic days — arguably higher total load than a
long run — plus a big eccentric strength stimulus. What they do **not** maintain is
running economy, leg turnover, and LT/MP specificity. So the block's job is: **hold
the aerobic base (hiking does most of it), keep the running stride awake with short
easy runs + strides, and above all not get injured.**

**#1 risk — eccentric descent load, not fitness loss.** Pip Ivan (+1200 m down) and
Shpytsi (+750 m down) fall two days apart. That is far more eccentric calf/quad damage
than this athlete's normal running, landing on top of the *active soleus flag* and the
known 2–3×/year shin/calf-syndrome history. The hikes are the injury exposure of the
whole trip. Non-negotiable guards:
- **Trekking poles on every descent** (already in the hike notes — this is the single
  biggest lever to cut eccentric leg load).
- **Do not run hard the day before or the day after a big hike.** The built-in rest/
  recovery days (Aug 24 banya, Aug 26 Bukovel, Aug 27 travel) are training — protect them.
- Monitor soleus/shin **daily**; anything that alters gait → stop stacking running on
  top of hiking, drop the run, keep hiking only if pain-free.
- Keep the daily calf/soleus/tibialis protocol running through the whole trip.

## Shin Splint Protocol (standing rule)

Any shin soreness at or above 2/10 during or after a run:
1. Skip the next quality session; replace with easy 30 min
2. Report to coach before resuming threshold/interval work
3. If soreness persists 3+ days: pause all running, refer to physio
4. Do not "run through it" — every week of early intervention saves 3 weeks of forced rest
