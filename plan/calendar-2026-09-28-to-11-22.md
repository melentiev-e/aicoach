## Weekly workout structure

- Monday: short Recovery, or rest when needed after Sunday. September 28 is already completed.
- Tuesday: main quality workout; allow about 3 km Easy warm-up and at least 2 km Easy cool-down, all inside the weekly total.
- Wednesday: Easy, usually 8–12 km. Keep it short after a demanding Tuesday.
- Thursday: aerobic medium-long Easy run, progressing roughly 10–12 to 14–16 km. Week 6 substitutes the short threshold session, including warm-up/cool-down, for this easy run rather than adding another run.
- Friday: Recovery, usually 4–8 km, or rest. Do not redistribute skipped distance.
- Saturday: Easy, usually 8–12 km; preserve freshness for Sunday.
- Sunday: the listed long run, entirely Easy. A 30–32 km run is already a substantial stressor even at easy pace.

Use the remaining easy/recovery kilometres to reach the week's target only when recovery supports it. Example 100 km distribution, Monday through Sunday: 8 + 14 (threshold session total) + 12 + 16 + 6 + 12 + 32 = 100 km. This is an allocation example, not an instruction to force the target. No doubles are required; an established easy day can be split if that makes the duration manageable, without adding distance.

- Easy and long: current reference 4:30–4:55/km, HR <148, genuinely conversational RPE 2–3. Start long runs near the slower end; run slower than the band when needed. HR is a ceiling, not a target.
- Recovery: reference 5:00–5:30/km, HR <132, RPE 1–2. Walk breaks are fine. Comfortable low effort matters more than squeezing into a pace band.
- Threshold: retain provisional 3:46–3:53/km; begin near 3:53. RPE about 6, controlled breathing, no accumulating race effort. HR 158–165 is supporting information after it settles, not something to chase. Slow down or shorten when effort does not match. The recent 3:35–3:40 session at RPE 7–8 does not justify resetting threshold faster.
- VO₂max-oriented intervals: time-based, RPE 7–8, strong but repeatable. Finish able to complete another repetition; no all-out last rep. Use a measured route and lap-average pace where available, not instantaneous GPS. No fixed pace or HR target until current workout evidence supports one; the historical 3:18–3:28 band is not being prescribed here.
- Strides: smooth and relaxed, not maximal; stop if form tightens. Full recovery between repetitions.

The large majority of running remains easy; at least roughly 80–90% of running time at genuinely easy/recovery effort is a practical intent, not a quota requiring more hard running. These short quality doses leave substantial easy volume. Exact physiological time in zones cannot be inferred from session titles or average HR.

## Progression gates

Advance only when the previous week's mileage is comfortable, there is no new pain, fatigue resolves between sessions, sleep is satisfactory, and easy/long running stays conversational without a repeat RPE 5–6 finish. HRV is contextual: look for a sustained personal trend alongside symptoms and effort, not an isolated cutoff.

If those conditions fail, repeat the last comfortable week or reduce it by approximately 15–25%; drop the second quality workout first. Do not make up missed kilometres. Keep planned recovery weeks even when the build runs behind; delay the 100 km/32 km milestones instead of compressing them.

Advance long runs only after the preceding distance is comfortable, including the next morning. If 26 km is demanding, repeat or shorten it rather than progressing to 28. Weather, duty load or poor sleep can reduce any session. New shin/calf pain means stop progression and reassess; gait-changing or persistent pain needs professional assessment.

For long runs, rehearse familiar gels/fluids from the start rather than waiting until depleted. Keep familiar calf/soleus/tibialis maintenance light; this build is not the time to add heavy unfamiliar strength work.

## Evidence and limits

Individual recovery and recent load govern this plan, consistent with [AIS guidance](https://www.ausport.gov.au/ais/position_statements/training-load-in-relation-to-loading-and-unloading-phases-of-training). A [5200-runner cohort](https://bjsm.bmj.com/content/early/2025/07/07/bjsports-2024-109380?versioned=true) links single-session distance spikes to injury risk; the staged long-run progression is a precaution, not a validated safe threshold for this athlete. The exact eight-week sequence is a coaching proposal, not a research-proven route to 100 km.
